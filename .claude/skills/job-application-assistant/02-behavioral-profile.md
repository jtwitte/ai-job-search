## Overview
Jan-Torben's behavioral profile is based on self-assessment rather than a formal instrument. He describes himself as a thorough, reliable, and collaborative professional who thrives on hands-on fieldwork paired with cross-disciplinary problem-solving, but who is not the strongest solo/independent coder and does his best work with regular technical exchange from colleagues.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Reliability / Conscientiousness | High | Thorough, attention to detail, follows through; willing to put in extra hours when a project requires it |
| Collaboration / Team Orientation | High | Prefers working with others, enjoys bringing people together, thrives on exchange with colleagues |
| Field / Mobility Orientation | High | Enjoys being in different places for data acquisition or project collaboration; comfortable with travel |
| Independent Technical Problem-Solving | Moderate-Low | Not the strongest solo coder; prefers a collaborative sounding board for coding difficulties rather than working in isolation |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Reliability & thoroughness:** Consistently detail-oriented and dependable; willing to invest extra effort when a project genuinely needs it
- **Cross-functional communication:** Adapts and relates well to different characters/stakeholders; comfortable explaining technical work to non-specialist audiences (e.g. the Fraunhofer IOSB Ruhr River video feature)
- **Team-building / connector:** Enjoys bringing people together and fostering collaboration across disciplines and institutions (e.g. interim lead of the cross-institutional Smart Ocean Technologies working group)

## How You Work Best
- Field-based or hands-on environments that mix sensor/vehicle data acquisition with desk-based analysis and stakeholder communication
- Collaborative teams with room for technical exchange, rather than isolated individual-contributor work
- Environments spanning multiple disciplines (mechanical, programming, organisational) where variety keeps him engaged
- Working alongside other experts, with opportunities to travel between sites/projects

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Independent coding/debugging:** Not the strongest standalone programmer. Frame as: values pair-programming and team-based development, and is actively strengthening this through the Python CS50 course and a planned C course.
- **Owning an entire technical problem solo:** Prefers to bring in the right specialist or collaborate rather than working through everything alone. Frame as: a natural fit for cross-functional teams and a strength in coordination/integration roles, rather than pure solo individual-contributor positions.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "cross-disciplinary team", "multi-disciplinary project"
- "field work", "hands-on", "site visits", "travel required"
- "collaborative environment", "stakeholder communication"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "independent contributor", "work autonomously with minimal support"
- "sole owner of the codebase", "solo development role"
- "extensive long-term offshore rotations" (soft preference against, not a hard deal-breaker - see Deal-breakers in CLAUDE.md)

## Management Style Preferences
- Works well with managers who involve him in team coordination and value his communication/organisational strengths
- Appreciates access to technical colleagues for troubleshooting rather than being left to solve complex coding problems entirely alone
- Responds well to being trusted with interim leadership/coordination responsibility (demonstrated as interim lead of the SOT working group)

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasize reliability, the full field-to-analysis pipeline (data acquisition -> problem-solving -> analysis -> stakeholder presentation), and communicator/connector strengths.
- **CV:** Emphasize hands-on fieldwork combined with end-to-end ownership of the data pipeline, and interdisciplinary team leadership (SOT interim lead).
- **Interviews:** Lean on the SOT interim-lead and Ruhr River media-feature examples to demonstrate communication and coordination strengths (see 07-interview-prep.md).
- **Don't overstate:** Avoid presenting as an independent, solo senior software developer. Frame coding as one part of a broader systems-integration and fieldwork skill set, and be upfront that collaborative development suits him better than isolated ownership of a codebase.
