## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Ruhr River Autonomous Survey (Stakeholder Communication & Field Leadership)
**S:** Fraunhofer IOSB was contracted by the Düsseldorf Regional Government (Bezirksregierung) to survey a 6 km stretch of the Ruhr River to support river maintenance and flood-event calculations.
**T:** As part of the Smart Ocean Technologies group, responsible for conducting the on-site measurements and explaining the methodology and findings to stakeholders.
**A:** Operated an autonomous surface vehicle ("Otter") equipped with sonar, LiDAR, and camera sensors to fully automate bathymetric and riverbank surveying; served as the on-site technical expert, explaining the process in a Fraunhofer IOSB video feature.
**R:** Delivered a comprehensive, precise hydrographic point-cloud dataset supporting the regional authority's river maintenance and flood-modelling work; featured on Fraunhofer IOSB's LinkedIn as the technical voice of the project.
**Use for:** "Tell me about explaining technical work to a non-technical audience", "Describe a time you represented your team/organisation publicly"

### 2. Autonomous Mapping Publication (Cross-Site Collaboration & Technical Writing)
**S:** Fraunhofer IOSB wanted to demonstrate that its Autonomy Toolbox (ATB) algorithms, developed for larger platforms, could transfer to a small uncrewed surface vehicle.
**T:** Contributed as co-author alongside four Fraunhofer IOSB colleagues across the Rostock and Karlsruhe sites, designing and documenting multi-domain mapping trials.
**A:** Ran field trials across harbours and lakes, fusing georeferenced LiDAR, sonar, and camera data; co-wrote the resulting paper for peer review.
**R:** Published in the Journal of Applied Hydrography (HN129, 2024), showing that ASV autonomy functions are practical for real-world use (while manual verification is still needed) - directly informing the team's ongoing product direction.
**Use for:** "Describe a time you collaborated across teams/sites", "Tell me about a technical result you're proud of"

### 3. MPhil Thesis Industry Interest (Bridging Research and Industry)
**S:** During the MPhil at the University of Southampton, developed a coupled CFD and observational approach to improve turbulence measurements from ocean gliders.
**T:** Needed to validate and communicate the method's relevance beyond academic supervisors, including to project collaborators and industry contacts.
**A:** Worked with Rockland Scientific, a project collaborator, on the measurement approach, and separately discussed the method with a software engineer at Alseamar, including in person at Oceanology 2024.
**R:** Generated genuine industry interest in a turbulence-measurement approach developed as part of an academic thesis - evidence the work has practical, not just academic, value.
**Use for:** "Tell me about research that had real-world impact", "Why should we care about your academic background for an industry role?"

### 4. Interim Lead, Smart Ocean Technologies Working Group (Leadership & Coordination)
**S:** Fraunhofer IOSB's cross-institutional Smart Ocean Technologies (SOT) working group needed interim leadership for six months.
**T:** Acted as interim lead (01/2021-06/2021), responsible for coordinating interdisciplinary collaboration and supporting strategic project development.
**A:** Restructured team workflows and coordinated across disciplines and institutions to keep the group's initiatives moving.
**R:** Maintained group cohesion and project momentum through the interim period, then returned to a technical individual-contributor role.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you led a team", "Describe stepping up into a leadership role you weren't originally hired for"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave Fraunhofer IOSB?"
> After five years developing and field-testing autonomous maritime systems at Fraunhofer IOSB, I decided it was the right time for a professional reorientation - to look more broadly across research, government, and industry for the role that best fits where I want to take my career next, while also completing further training (GIS, Python) to broaden my skill set. I'm not leaving the field, I'm looking for the right next step within it.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> Acknowledge the specific gap honestly, then bridge: for coding-heavy gaps specifically - "I'm not the strongest solo software engineer - I do my best work with some team exchange rather than working entirely in isolation. I'm actively closing that gap through the Python CS50 course and a planned C course, and my systems-integration background means I pick up new tools quickly in practice." For other gaps, tailor similarly: name the closest adjacent experience and a concrete step already underway to close it.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Continuing to grow within the marine technology / maritime robotics space - either as a senior technical specialist bridging field data acquisition and stakeholder-facing analysis, or in a coordination/leadership role like the one I held as interim lead of the Smart Ocean Technologies group, depending on where the next opportunity takes me.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I'm not the strongest independent software developer - I do my best work when I can exchange ideas with colleagues rather than solving complex coding problems entirely alone. I'm mitigating this by actively building my coding skills (Python CS50, a planned C course) and by making a point of working collaboratively, which also tends to produce better results.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
